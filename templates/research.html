The research behind EyeQuest

EyeQuest hasn't been tested in any study, and there's no evidence yet that it improves vision. This page summarizes published research that EyeQuest's design draws on, including results that don't support game-based treatment. It isn't medical advice. A lazy eye (amblyopia) should be treated with an eye doctor.

The short version

  1. Patching works, but children get far less of it than prescribed. How much vision improves depends on how many hours the patch is actually worn.
  2. Games on their own have had mixed results. In the largest trial, an iPad game did slightly worse than patching, partly because few children played as much as prescribed.
  3. Some carefully designed digital treatments have done well in trials. They show each eye different images through special glasses or a headset, which EyeQuest doesn't do.
  4. Doing an activity while patched doesn't seem to speed up recovery on its own. The patch does the work. A game's value is getting the patch worn, and showing that it was.

So EyeQuest's goal is narrow and testable: help children do the practice their eye doctor prescribed, and record that it happened. Whether it does that is still unproven.

What EyeQuest hasn't shown

  • That it improves vision.
  • That children use it more, or patch for longer, than they would without it.
  • That its eye tracking is accurate for anyone besides its developer. The best result so far missed by 6.6% of the screen's height, for one person on one camera.
  • That Gap Spotter's "smallest ring" measures how well you see. It isn't a validated vision test.

How EyeQuest could be tested

Any study with children would need an ethics board's approval, parents' consent, and an eye doctor running it.

  1. Tracking check: compare EyeQuest with a research-grade eye tracker on 10–20 adults.
  2. Usability pilot: children already prescribed patching use EyeQuest for a few weeks, and a patch-wear sensor checks whether its records are accurate.
  3. Adherence trial: patching with EyeQuest versus patching alone, measuring hours actually patched.
  4. Vision trial: only after those succeed, measure vision with examiners who don't know which group each child is in.
