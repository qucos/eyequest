# The research behind EyeQuest

**EyeQuest has not been tested in any study, and there is no evidence yet that it improves vision.** This page summarizes published research that EyeQuest's design draws on, including results that don't support game-based treatment. It is not medical advice. Amblyopia (lazy eye) should be treated under an eye doctor's care.

## The short version

1. **Patching works, but children get far less of it than prescribed.** How much vision improves depends on how many hours the patch is actually worn.
2. **Games on their own have had mixed results.** In the largest trial, an iPad game did slightly worse than patching, partly because few children played as much as prescribed.
3. **Some carefully designed digital treatments have done well in trials.** These show each eye different images using special glasses or a headset, which EyeQuest does not do.
4. **Doing an activity while patched doesn't seem to speed up recovery on its own.** The patch does the work. A game's value is in getting children to wear the patch and in measuring that they did.

So EyeQuest's goal is narrow and testable: **help children do the practice their eye doctor prescribed, and record that it happened.** Whether it achieves that is still unproven.

## 1. Adherence is the central problem

- **More patching gives more improvement, up to a point.** In the Monitored Occlusion Treatment of Amblyopia Study (MOTAS), a sensor recorded when 94 children actually wore their patches. Vision gains rose steadily with the total hours patched. On average, children wore the patch for 48% of the prescribed time, and most of the improvement came within 6 weeks.
  *Stewart CE, et al. Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci. 2004;45:3048–3054. [doi:10.1167/iovs.04-0250](https://doi.org/10.1167/iovs.04-0250)*
- **Children wear patches for less time than prescribed.** In a randomized trial of 80 children, those prescribed 6 hours a day wore the patch for 4.2 hours on average, and those prescribed 12 hours wore it for 6.2. Children who wore it for less than 3 hours a day did significantly worse than those who wore it longer.
  *Stewart CE, et al. BMJ. 2007;335:707. [doi:10.1136/bmj.39301.460150.55](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.39301.460150.55)*

**What this means for EyeQuest:** patch time that actually happens is what matters, and it's hard to know how much happened. EyeQuest's streaks are designed to encourage it, and its solo-eye tracking is designed to check that the lazy eye was doing the work. Neither has been tested.

## 2. Game-based treatment: mixed results

- **iPad game vs. patching, ages 5–12 (385 children).** After 16 weeks, vision improved 1.05 lines with the game and 1.35 lines with 2 hours of daily patching. The game didn't meet the trial's test for being "not meaningfully worse" than patching. Only 22% of children played more than 75% of the prescribed time.
  *Holmes JM, et al. JAMA Ophthalmol. 2016;134:1391–1400. [doi:10.1001/jamaophthalmol.2016.4262](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaophthalmol.2016.4262)*
- **iPad game vs. glasses alone, ages 7–12 previously treated (138 children).** The game gave no added benefit to vision after 4 or 8 weeks.
  *Pediatric Eye Disease Investigator Group (PEDIG). Ophthalmology. 2019;126:456–466. [doi:10.1016/j.ophtha.2018.10.032](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ophtha.2018.10.032)*
- **Cochrane systematic review (2022).** Based on the one randomized trial that met its criteria, the reviewers were moderately confident that this kind of game-based treatment gives gains comparable to patching after 16 weeks. They said evidence was too limited for firm conclusions and called for longer follow-up.
  *Tailor V, et al. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2022;2:CD011347. [doi:10.1002/14651858.CD011347.pub3](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011347.pub3)*

## 3. Digital treatments that did well in trials

These products show each eye different images. They work differently from EyeQuest, so their results do not carry over to it.

- **Luminopia**, which shows each eye a different version of TV shows in a headset: 105 children aged 4–7. After 12 weeks, vision improved 1.8 lines with the treatment plus glasses, compared with 0.8 lines with glasses alone. The trial was stopped early because the result was already clear.
  *Xiao S, et al. Ophthalmology. 2022;129:77–85. [doi:10.1016/j.ophtha.2021.09.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ophtha.2021.09.001)*
  The company reports FDA authorization in 2021 ([announcement](https://eyewire.news/news/luminopia-announces-fda-approval-of-digital-therapeutic-to-improve-vision-in-children-with-lazy-eye)).
- **CureSight**, which uses **eye tracking** during a two-eye treatment: 197 children aged 4 to under 9, across three countries. It was not meaningfully worse than patching overall, and it did better than patching among children who completed at least 60% of the prescribed time.
  *Yehezkel O, et al. Sci Rep. 2026. [doi:10.1038/s41598-026-60632-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-60632-5)*
  The company reports FDA 510(k) clearance in 2022 ([announcement](https://eyewire.news/news/novasight-announces-fda-510k-clearance-of-curesight-digital-therapy-for-amblyopia)).

**What this means for EyeQuest:** eye tracking can be part of a successful home treatment, and engaging, well-designed digital treatment can improve vision. EyeQuest uses an ordinary webcam instead of special hardware, and it isn't a two-eye treatment.

## 4. Activities during patching

- **Near vs. distance activities while patched (children aged 3–6, with 2 hours of daily patching).** After 8 weeks, vision improved 2.5 lines with near activities such as games and reading, and 2.6 lines with distance activities. Near activities did not improve results.
  *PEDIG. Ophthalmology. 2008;115:2071–2078. [doi:10.1016/j.ophtha.2008.06.031](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ophtha.2008.06.031)*

**What this means for EyeQuest:** EyeQuest's solo-eye games shouldn't be expected to work better than patching alone. Their realistic value is making the patch time happen and showing that it did.

## What EyeQuest has not shown

- That it improves vision.
- That children use it more, or patch for longer, than they would without it.
- That its eye tracking is accurate for anyone besides its developer. The best live result so far missed by 6.6% of the screen's height, from one person on one camera.
- That Gap Spotter's "smallest ring" measures visual acuity. It isn't a validated vision test.

## How EyeQuest could be tested

Any study with children would need an ethics board's approval, parents' consent, and an eye doctor running it. These steps are listed in order of difficulty:

1. **Tracking check (adults, no medical claims):** compare EyeQuest's gaze readings with a research-grade eye tracker on 10–20 people, to measure real accuracy across faces, lighting and cameras.
2. **Usability and adherence pilot:** children already prescribed patching use EyeQuest for a few weeks. Measure how many days they practice, and compare EyeQuest's session logs with a patch-wear sensor to see whether the logs are accurate.
3. **Randomized adherence trial:** compare patching with EyeQuest against patching alone, with hours actually patched as the main measure.
4. **Vision outcome trial:** only after the earlier steps succeed, measure vision with masked examiners, as in the trials above.
